01

Brand

ExtraliteStudio Riva · Northern Italy · Since 1995

We treat every gram
as a matter of faith.

Extralite is an ultralight racing parts brand founded in Italy in 1995. The company is Studio Riva, led by Sergio Riva. Its first goal was a full-suspension mountain bike frameset under 2kg, rear hydraulic shock included.

Building parts to suit that frame, one by one, the range grew to stems, hubs, cranks and wheels, and by the late 1990s Extralite had completed a full-suspension bike under 8kg. Every product is still designed, machined and assembled in northern Italy.

No Frills, Just Substance

No frills, just substance.
Materials and machining over decoration.

Extralite puts its research money into the best materials and machining equipment. Parts cut on large multi-axis CNC machines are assembled one at a time by Italian technicians with prototype-level care, and weights are published honestly. The part you receive may even be lighter than listed.

The lighter the part, the longer the testing. HyperStem went through months of fatigue testing and exceeded the standard by a factor of four, so it is recommended even for enduro. Most parts made since 1995 can still be serviced with spare parts and restoration.

MachiningOne hour per stem

Each HyperStem is 3D machined for over an hour on a Mazak multi-axis machine. Even the surfaces are machined smooth to reduce stress concentrations.

TestingTested to boredom

Key products are fatigue tested beyond UNI and EN standards. QRC-3 cranks are 17% lighter and 15% stiffer than the previous generation.

BearingsMicro-tuner

The micro-tuner sets hub bearing preload from the outside. Bearings range from ABI-Enduro steel to HyperCeramic and XD-15 ceramic.

ServiceBuilt to be serviced

Spare parts are supplied down to a single bolt or pawl. Built light, and built to be serviced for years.

Holistic Engineering

Not one part,
but the whole bike.

Extralite started with a frame, so it never looks at a part in isolation. Stems have rounded clamp edges and lower recommended torque to protect carbon bars and steerers, and hubs have flanges and spoke holes designed around the spokes and rims of the finished wheel.

Weight comes off for faster acceleration, climbing and handling, while the stiffness and durability needed for real racing stay. That is why cross-country racers have used Extralite parts for years.

Cockpit

HyperStem · HyperBar · UltraTopStems, bars, headsets

An hour of machining
leaves 54 grams.

In 1997 Extralite introduced The Stem, with a four-bolt faceplate. It weighed 128g at 120mm. Through several generations it became today's HyperStem, and the same 110mm now weighs 83g.

HyperStem is 3D machined from a 7075 aluminium block on a multi-axis machine for over an hour. The bolts are grade 5 titanium Torx with rounded heads, and every clamp edge is rounded to protect carbon bars and steerers. Recommended torque is 2Nm.

03

Hubs

Hyper · CyberOff-road and road hub families

Hubs from the lightest end
that keep on turning.

Extralite hubs come in two families. Hyper, for mountain and gravel, uses an all-alloy front-engagement freehub to handle high torque and mud, a thick 17mm axle and external seals. Cyber, for the road, saves weight with a titanium ratchet, two pawls and four bearings.

Both families set bearing preload from the outside with the micro-tuner. Bearings range from standard ABI-Enduro steel to stainless, HyperCeramic and Enduro XD-15 ceramic.

Bearings

Choose the bearings
to complete the hub.

The same hub changes weight, feel and price with its bearings. ABI-Enduro ABEC5 steel is standard, 440C stainless suits riders who see rain and sweat, and the smoothest, rust-free choices are HyperCeramic and Enduro XD-15 ceramic.

Wheels

CyberDisc · CyberClimb · HyperGravel · CarboCamberCarbon wheelsets on Extralite hubs

Sub-kilogram wheels,
built on our own hubs.

Over a decade ago Extralite introduced the offset carbon rim and hookless rim walls on the CarboCamber29, and today's rims have even more offset and wider beads. Every spoke hole on the rim is individually reinforced and reworked so each nipple aligns with its spoke.

Spokes can be carbon, Berd fibre or steel. Light rims reduce rotational inertia for quicker acceleration and changes of direction.

Drivetrain · Frames

QRC-3S · OctaPhase2 · HyperBBCranks, rings, bearings, pulleys

Machining once reserved for prototypes,
on every crank.

The QRC crank began with a design that hollows the section and distributes thickness along the stress path. Today's QRC-3 is 17% lighter and 15% stiffer than the previous generation. Each crankset spends hours on a five-axis machine with point-to-point 3D machining, the care once given only to prototypes.

Chainrings, bottom brackets, pulleys, quick releases and thru axles follow the same principle. Ceramic bearings go where drivetrain friction matters most, and weight is cut to single-digit grams.
